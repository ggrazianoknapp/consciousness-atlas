What this is

A reading aid across six texts on AI consciousness. Each theory card gives one neutral summary, then what each source says about the view and its implications for AI, in that source's own framing. Where a source names a theory but is silent on AI, the card says so instead of filling the gap. Nothing here is endorsed by the authors.

How the axes were chosen

Rows are the theory's own verdict on AI consciousness, as the sources report it — not any author's verdict. Columns are what the theory takes reality to be made of, following the FAU primer's monism/dualism tree, plus a column for mechanism-level theories and cross-cutting hypotheses (substrate independence, functionalism) that are silent on metaphysics. Saad & Mogensen's point runs through the whole grid: what matters for AI is the basis of consciousness, not its nature, so dualist and physicalist views alike can land in "it depends".

Placement judgement calls

  • Computational functionalism is "yes" because that is what the view implies; Seth's case that the view is false appears on its card, not as a different row.
  • GWT, higher-order theories, AST are "yes" on Butlin et al.'s computational reading — conditional on computational functionalism. FAU's doubt about GWT in silicon is a doubt about that condition.
  • IIT is split from the FAU primer's "informational theories" because Butlin et al. and Tononi himself treat it as denying consciousness to conventional digital computers.
  • Predictive processing, RPT, embodied/agency views are "it depends" because the sources pull in opposite directions: Butlin et al. derive computational indicators from them; Seth reads them as evidence for biological naturalism.
  • Substrate independence is a hypothesis about basis rather than a theory, so it sits in the cross-cutting column.
