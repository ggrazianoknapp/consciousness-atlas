Consciousness Atlas

The theories of consciousness in Schneider, Sahner, Kuhn, Bailey & Schwitzgebel (2025), Is AI Conscious? A Primer on the Myths and Confusions Driving the Debate (FAU white paper), mapped by what each says reality is made of and whether an AI could be conscious.

Rows: the theory's verdict on AI consciousness, as the paper reports it. Columns: the theory's view of reality. Tap a theory for details.

The paper's own ordering: a superordinate split between monism and dualism, with physicalist and non-physicalist monisms underneath. Mechanism-level theories appear elsewhere in the paper and are grouped separately here.

Section II of the paper: the conceptual distinctions whose conflation drives most confusion about AI consciousness.

What this is

A reading aid. Every theory card paraphrases what the white paper says about that view and its implications for AI consciousness. Where the paper names a theory but does not say what it implies for AI, the card says so instead of filling the gap.

How the axes were chosen

The paper organises theories by metaphysics (monism vs dualism, physicalist vs not) and, for each, reports a stance on AI consciousness — usually "in principle yes", "no", or "it depends on what our best science can do". Those two dimensions are the grid. The paper's headline observation is visible in it: most views are neutral; the firm "no" comes from biological naturalism and eliminativism, the firm optimism from computational functionalism.

Placement judgement calls

  • Informational theories sit under physicalist monism because the paper puts them there, though it notes information is treated as more fundamental than matter.
  • Quantum theories can be physicalist or not; the paper files them as non-physicalist because the named versions lean on panpsychism.
  • Mysterianism is placed in the physicalist column per the paper, with its own caveat that a mysterian could be agnostic.
  • Mechanism-level theories (GWT, HOT, Local Recurrence, embodied/enactive, etc.) are not in the paper's Section III taxonomy; they come from Sections I–II and get their own column.
